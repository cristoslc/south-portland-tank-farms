§5 / methodology claims

FC-5.1 — Claim: DEP license terms are 5 or 10 years with evergreen extension. Source: each license order + 5 M.R.S. §10002 + 06-096 C.M.R. ch. 140. Direct evidence: every order contains “The term of this license shall be five/ten (5/10) years from the signature date above,” followed by the note: “If a complete renewal application … is submitted at least six but no more than 18 months prior to expiration … all terms and conditions of the Part 70 license shall remain in effect until the Department takes final action on the … renewal application.” — ✅

FC-5.2 — Claim: DEP’s 2021 legislative report exists and responded to L.D. 1915. Source: DEP report “Measurement and Control of Emissions from Aboveground Petroleum Storage Tanks” (Jan 2021), maine.gov/dep/publications/reports/ (on file as MEDEP_tank_report_2021.pdf). Direct evidence: “In the second regular session of 2020, the 129th Maine Legislature passed L.D. 1915: ‘Resolve, Directing the Department of Environmental Protection To Evaluate Emissions from Aboveground Petroleum Storage Tanks.’” — ✅

FC-5.3 — Claim: DEP does not currently require direct stack testing of tank emissions; relies on operator estimates (AP-42). Source: DEP 2021 report. Direct evidence: “5. Stack Testing Methods The Department recommends and supports the development of an EPA standard test method … to quantify emissions from” (heated tanks; i.e., no standard method exists and none is required yet); report’s emissions-estimation chapter describes AP-42 calculation as the basis of emissions inventories. — ✅

FC-5.4 — Claim: DEP declined to recommend fenceline monitoring in its 2021 report. Source: DEP 2021 report. Direct evidence: “2. Fenceline Monitoring The Department does not recommend fenceline monitoring at this time. Fenceline monitoring is most commonly used around the perimeter of large facilities … Given the proximity of petroleum stor[age facilities]…” — ✅

FC-6 / claims REMOVED with the PSP packet (no longer sourced in memo)

The following claims appeared in earlier drafts citing the Protect South Portland info packet; they are no longer used as factual support in the memo. Where a fact was needed, it was re-verified from an official source; where none exists, the claim was dropped or explicitly labeled as advocacy material.

  • “671 tons VOC+HAP permitted emissions” — replaced with per-license official limits (FC-1.7). The 671 figure was a third-party compilation (Falatko table) mixing permitted and other-industry totals; the DEP’s own report does not state it.
  • “Tanks border Kaler on two sides” / Ligonia daycare testimony / SP High photo — dropped (advocacy testimony; not verifiable). The measured distances (Kaler→PPLC 0.04 mi, Growing Learners→Sprague 0.00 mi) stand on the OSM/GIS analysis alone.
  • “95% control technology exists” — dropped (vendor/advocacy claim, not verified).
  • “Consent decrees did not mandate controls” — not carried forward; consent-decree terms were not independently reviewed in this project. (EPA consent decree pages exist for Global and Sprague; a future revision could verify this directly from EPA’s settlement pages.)
  • PPLC “largest permitted HAP emitter” historical ranking; “6–8 of 23 tanks refilled” — dropped; the Feb 2025 PPLC order (FC-1.6) is now the sole basis for PPLC’s limits and throughput.
  • “Stack testing at next license renewal” recommendation — re-framed as the community’s ask (§7), not as an official recommendation; the DEP report actually recommended developing an EPA test method (FC-5.3).
